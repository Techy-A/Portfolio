Kaggle, 2026
SHL Hiring Assessment

Scoring spoken grammar from 60 seconds of audio

The model listens to 45 to 60 seconds of spontaneous spoken English and predicts a grammar score from 0 to 5. The hard part is that speech recognisers correct grammar by default, which removes the very errors the model has to score.

Each clip is judged three ways: what was said (verbatim Whisper transcripts into DeBERTa-v3-large embeddings), how it was said (frozen WavLM and Whisper-encoder embeddings, which turned out to be the strongest signal), and what an audio language model hears (Voxtral-Mini-3B hidden states). A non-negative blend combines them, and a WavLM gate sets the clips that score zero.

validation

Speakers repeat

Training clips come in speaker twins, so random folds let a model recognise the voice. Every fold is speaker-grouped and tuned to sit as far from training as the test set does.

distribution shift

Test clips are shorter

71% of test clips run 55 seconds or less, against 26% in training. Training on test-length crops was the biggest single gain: 0.3454 to 0.3350.

metric probing

The metric is not pure RMSE

Two probe submissions exposed a Pearson component and a test-set bias of +0.118. The predicted score after removing it was 0.3259 to 0.3264. The actual result was 0.3262.
